Once upon a time, there was a town, and in the town lived a baker's son called Andrew and a fuller's daughter called Beatrice. They first met each other when they were playing in the garden of their mothers' friend. He smelled like raisin and parmesan and quite like how the girls who smelled smelled, only they smelled more often of magnolia and honeysuckle and pears heavy on the branch. He did not have his father's strong arms, and made pastries while his brothers kneaded.

When the town's boys would tease and fight him, her footsteps became loud and the boys would scatter, and when she took him away she smelled of a sour burn.

When he became a young man, he would sneak into the oven half a dozen of his tarts and put them in a basket and bring it to Beatrice. He liked how she smelled of smoke and wool and a hint of wine when she ate his tarts. It was but a little dough and a dash of preserves, so Andrew's father did not mind. He had realized that a girl like Beatrice would make Andrew happy.

Beatrice had quick hands and the mill grew busy, Andrew grew into patience and made pastries for the lords and for the festivals.

The lord mused about bringing him to the castle so he could better bake for the nobles.

It was her seventeenth winter when the fire in her whispered too loud in her ear, the smell of Andrew in his butter and cinnamon roiled in her mind and she could hear in her ear a plea to *bite him and never again fear his flight.*

Beatrice was a smart girl, and went to a midwife to ask for her wisdom.

The midwife turned to vinegar and held her shoulders, she said the following.

*My heart, the boy smells of butter and raisins because he lives with his brothers in the bakery, his scent is not his but of his home. I have seen the boy and he does not have the warm blood that overflows into blooming honeysuckle and freshly made malt. If you wound him at the neck, his cool blood would fight the wound and grow cold and die.*

She told Andrew of what the midwife said. And Andrew in his love answered thus:

*I would rather be yours and sick than unscathed and away in the keep. Bite me tonight and grant me your union.*

By candlelight she was in his room, and he held her hand as her jaw neared his life, she smelled of the blacksmith's kiln.

She poured wine on the shallow cut, by morning the midwife had come, and lathered the wound in honey.

*He is of cooler blood, the guest is unbidden and the search will sear.*

Andrew lied to Beatrice and said it did not hurt at all.

By the second night the cut was pink and warm, and the midwife set her strongest herbs.

By Sunday he could not stand, and by Wednesday he was cold.

The hollow at Beatrice's throat stayed tender, and in the dry seasons it would chafe with every swallow. She wore a high collar for the rest of her days.

*My heart, be not like Andrew and Beatrice.*

*Do not let them love you that much.*
